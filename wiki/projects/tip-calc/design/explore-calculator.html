Frame
All to

Recommended: 2 · readout-first. It keeps the each-pays figure in sight above the phone keypad while you type, with the fewest controls and plain native inputs: the cheapest build, and the brief's numeric-keypad unlock still applies. What would change this: if you type, close the keypad and only then read, 1 · printed-slip is simpler and reads in check order; if the human-evidence gate shows the tip or head count changing more often than the bill, 3 · thumb-steppers.

Size. The brief names a phone held in one hand and a 320px floor, so every frame is portrait. 320 × 568 is assumed (the shortest 320-wide phone, where space is tightest); 390 × 844 is a common current phone. Frames are the whole screen in CSS pixels; the browser's own bars are not drawn, so a real page has roughly 100 to 150px less height than shown. In “Typing, keypad up” the phone's keypad is drawn at about 260px (300px on the large frame).

Fixed, not explored: the ledger-slip system in DESIGN.md (type, colour, spacing, radius, motion). The variants change the shape: where the answer sits, how tip and people go in, and whether the phone keypad opens. Mockups are static; switch states with the buttons. Every figure is computed with the brief's rule: integer cents, tip to the nearest cent, each share rounded up.

1

printed-slip

answer last, read top to bottom

Optimizes for
Reading it like the check: bill, tip, total, then each, so the rounding line sits under the arithmetic that caused it.
Costs
The answer is the last row. On a 320 × 568 phone the keypad hides it while you type, so it only "changes under your thumb" after you close the keypad; on 390 × 844 the figure just clears the keypad. At 320 × 568 the page only just fits: a rounding note makes it 3px too tall, an error message 43px.
First runnable version
The specimen's slip as one HTML file: three inputs, the preset row and a pure calc(); no new layout work.
2

readout-first

recommendedanswer pinned on top, fields are the ledger

Optimizes for
The memorable thing: the each-pays figure sits above every field and stays pinned, so it is visible over the phone keypad and changes with each digit.
Costs
The answer reads before its working (tip and total sit under the fields, not above the answer). The tip row packs three presets and the free field into one 4-up row of 58px cells at 320, the tightest fit on the page. The pinned readout keeps 96 to 150px of the roughly 300px left above the keypad on a 320 × 568 phone.
First runnable version
One page with native inputs (inputmode decimal and numeric), the readout as the first block with position: sticky, the same calc() as 1.
3

thumb-steppers

type the bill once, tap tip and people

Optimizes for
The brief's friction, re-typing when the tip or head count changes: after the bill, tip and people are minus and plus keys and presets in the bottom thumb zone, with no keypad.
Costs
Twice the controls (four step keys, three presets, three fields). From 18 to 25% is seven taps unless you type into the field. On a 390 × 844 phone the answer and the controls are about 490px apart.
First runnable version
Variant 2's page plus two stepper groups that write into the same fields; minus on People stops at 1.
4

own-keypad

calculator keys on the page

Optimizes for
"Like the phone's built-in Calculator": the page draws its own keys, so the phone keypad never opens, nothing jumps, and the answer and all three values stay on screen.
Costs
The most build: the keys replace native input, so screen readers, hardware keyboards, paste and the caret all need work by hand, and the brief's inputmode unlock no longer applies. At 320 × 568 it fills the screen to the pixel; an error message pushes the bottom key row 22px out of view.
First runnable version
Three row buttons (a radio group) choosing the active value, a 4 × 4 key grid appending to that value's string, the same calc().
